# Deep Forest C: design reasoning

This reasoning exists only for structured variant C. It translates the common
brief into spatial intent before choosing cells.

## Identity and rhythm

Mossveil Passage is a former wardens' trail through a forest that seems denser
than its actual topology. The route rhythm is:

`tight gate → crooked fern tunnel → fork → western hush loop → Bellroot reveal
→ rest clearing → rising north trail → last brush → threshold`.

The player should feel compression through tree framing, not through ambiguous
collision mazes. The pale critical path remains continuously legible.

## Spatial hierarchy

- The Bellroot Watcher is the single dominant landmark and traversal hinge.
- A broad central negative-space clearing makes that landmark visible after
  dense southern bends.
- The optional western loop leaves before the reveal and rejoins inside the
  clearing, so curiosity earns a different approach rather than a dead end.
- The secret sits on the loop's most remote bend and is telegraphed by a gap
  and a sign at the fork—not by random placement.
- Encounter cover occupies the damp western hollow and drier northern brush;
  the Bellroot clearing is explicitly safe.

## NPC logic

- A warden-naturalist studies the Bellroot clearing and anchors local story.
- A mushroom gatherer/trainer occupies the optional damp loop.
- A trail scout/trainer stands at the northern release, where a final challenge
  makes spatial and occupational sense.

## Frozen-vocabulary composition

Forest zones frame the left/right edges at different densities. Authored trees,
boulders, shrubs, flowers, and a sign establish meaningful silhouettes; seeded
scatter supplies only understory texture. No new art or compiler behavior is
requested.
